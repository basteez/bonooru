## 2. What went well

The owner's view: the same as Epic 2.

- **Measuring on the bench with real instruments** settled every acceptance criterion with a number:
  - the controller's own debug data (v5);
  - the multimeter on GP0, GP1 and 3V3;
  - the owner's ear for every one of the 40 A.3 rounds.
- **Retro action #27 paid off.** The bench-tool review and the dry run against `ocb-sim` before each bench caught three things before any owner time was spent:
  - the Enter-before-prompt bug (3.4);
  - a soak baseline taken from session start (3.4);
  - two AC numbers the tools could not measure (3.5).
- **The sans-IO core held.** The same `Session` drove the dry runs and the hardware. No link bug reached the bench.

## 3. Challenges

As in Epic 2, the problems sat in the tools and the process, not in the firmware.

- **Bench surprises the review and the dry run did not foresee:**
  - macOS completes the Pro Controller handshake by itself. The 3.1 H-row expected "not ready"; the retro of Epic 2 had already recorded this fact.
  - OoT's connection screen comes back after a Joy-Con hand-back. Our controller has to close it with A.
  - The console went to standby during a bench.
  - The OS kept a stale STATUS in the port's input buffer.
  - A SIGINT reached the agent's wrapper shell instead of the tool.
- **Tests that pass without testing.** Many IMPORTANT review findings were tests or verdicts that could not fail:
  - an OFF criterion that measured STATUS spacing;
  - a "neutral within 100 ms" that measured nothing;
  - assertions with no lower bound.
- **AC measurability found late.** That two AC numbers were not measurable surfaced only at the 3.5 code review. It cost a firmware change (debug STATUS v5) after the tooling was written.

## 6. Epic 4 preview

Epic 4 (M5): "App shows the link state and drives the Switch with the virtual gamepad". Stories 4.1 to 4.7 build the egui app shell, the serial worker and discovery, the three-LED status strip, the firmware version check and the STOP bar.

It builds on:
- `ocb-core`'s session (stable, used unchanged by `ocb-dev`);
- the own bridge's identity (`2E8A:000A`, "Bonooru Bridge", serial = flash ID);
- the UX decisions of this epic: a STATUS staleness of 150 ms drives the LEDs to `unknown` (Story 4.5), and `console_ready` keeps the 100 ms rule.

No significant discovery requires changing the Epic 4 plan. The cleanup story (action 4) comes first.

## 8. Key takeaways

1. Reviewing and dry-running the bench tools before the bench works: it moved several failures from the bench to the desk.
2. The same lesson came back from Epic 2: bench surprises live in the tools and in the console's behaviour, not in the firmware. Write them into the rig checklist.
3. Decide how each AC number is measured when the story is created, not when the tooling is reviewed.
